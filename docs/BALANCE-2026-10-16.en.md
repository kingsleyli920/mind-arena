# Yuan Shao takes the board alone: four interventions, all four failed, and why

## Conclusion

24 games with no season limit: **20 unified naturally, every one of them won by Yuan
Shao (袁绍)**; the remaining 4 ran the full 64 seasons without unifying, and in 3 of
those the leader was still Yuan Shao. In 1 of them (seed 22) it was Liu Zhang (刘璋)
with 56 commanderies against Yuan Shao's remaining 17. So it is not "Yuan Shao always
wins", it is "Yuan Shao almost always wins".

Two changes were made this round toward that end; together with two earlier changes
aimed at the same thing, **all four failed** — and we now know why: none of them
touched the binding constraint.

The quantity that actually matters is fixed at the opening — **neighbourhood
pressure**, i.e. "sum of the troops of all bordering factions ÷ one's own troops".
Cao Cao (曹操) has 21.86× his own troops next to him; Yuan Shao has only 1.57×, and
Yuan Shao simultaneously has the bulk of 13 commanderies and an average march cost of
4.1. That ratio is laid out by the scenario, and no **global** knob can move it: a knob
acts on every seat in the same direction, and this is a ratio *between* seats.

To be clear about one thing: **the sixteen-season evaluation is unaffected by any of
this.** The Spearman correlation between opening commandery count and sixteen-season
territory gain is **−0.13** — the faction handed a big opening does not gain an
advantage from it. The monopoly appears only in the long games, after the season limit
is removed. The acceptance line for the evaluation still holds; what does not hold is
"this scenario is suitable for repeated head-to-head play".

Zero model calls this round; model cost was zero. The working tree has been rolled back
to its pre-change state, and no unaccepted rule change has been left in the repo.

## Four failures

| Change | Expected | Measured | Why it failed (measured) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fortified-city threshold (content pack 1.2.0, earlier rounds) | Lengthen sieges | Did not meet the siege-duration criterion | Recorded separately at the time; the season limit was subsequently removed and siege duration resolved itself (0.07 → 0.61 seasons per siege) |
| Recurring coalitions (2.4.0, previous round) | Make the hegemon keep paying a price | 21/21 → 20/20 | Yuan Shao has no alliance to break, and the weak neighbours allying with each other still cannot muster the troops |
| Capture drops morale two tiers (this round, reverted) | Make conquest start costing orders | 20/24 → 20/24, median seasons 30 → 28 | **53.9% of city captures happen when the defender's morale is already ≤ tier 2**, so dropping it by 1 and dropping it by 2 hit the same floor (lower bound 1) |
| Four shapes of order-budget curve (this round, not landed) | Hold down the hegemon's action surface | Yuan Shao won 10–11 of 12, every time | Halving the slope only dragged unification from 28 seasons out to 40 — **different soup, same winner** |

The first two are negative results left over from earlier rounds. They are listed here
because they point at the same goal as this round's two, and **it takes all four
together to make the point**: it is not that some one knob was not turned far enough,
it is that this whole class of knob cannot reach.

### Why the digestion cost came out empty

The morale coefficient is `0.6 + tier/8`, which across its whole range is only 0.725 →
1.225. A tier is worth ten percent, and at the moment of capture more than half the
targets are already on the floor. The raw distribution is in
[reports/balance-2026-10-16/capture-morale-hist.txt](reports/balance-2026-10-16/capture-morale-hist.txt)
(Chinese).

Worth recording alongside that: a treasury of 19695 gold sits idle for long stretches.
**Gold is not a constraint in bot games**, because the bots barely buy anything. So the
diminishing-returns brake on gold is also spinning free at this scale.

## Which quantity actually explains it

Thirty factions, twelve games, territory gain at season 16, Spearman (n=30, SE≈0.19):

| Predictor | ρ |
|---|---:|
| Garrison per commandery (troop density) | **+0.61** |
| Sum of neighbours' troops ÷ own troops (neighbourhood pressure) | **−0.57** |
| Strongest neighbour's troops ÷ own troops | −0.52 |
| Opening garrison | +0.39 |
| Number of neighbours weaker than half of me | +0.14 |
| **Opening commandery count** | **−0.13** |
| Mean march cost to external neighbours | +0.07 |

With Yuan Shao removed as an outlier (it is +18.0; second place is +7.7), troop density
is still +0.61 — it is not being propped up by the outlier.

**I got this wrong partway through.** I first looked at the two-point comparison
between Yuan Shao (99644 troops, 12 external neighbours, mean cost 4.1, wins
everything) and Liu Biao (107189 troops, 12 external neighbours, mean cost 10.4, wins
nothing), and concluded that terrain advantage was the main cause. Computed across all
thirty factions, that judgment does not hold: the ρ for march cost is only +0.07. Lü Bu
(吕布) has the lowest mean cost on the whole map at 1.6 and eleven cheap directions,
and a sixteen-season gain of −3.8; Liu Bei (刘备) has cost 3.3, ten cheap directions,
and −5.2. Flat ground plus weak neighbours describes **the two factions that got eaten**
just as well.

Low pressure on its own is not enough: Han Sui's pressure is 1.00 but he has only 300
troops and 1 commandery, boxed in between the equally destitute Song Jian (宋建) and
the Qiang and Hu (羌胡). Liu Zhang's pressure is 1.55 with 104224 troops, but he is
shut in by the Qinling (秦岭) and Daba (大巴山) ranges and manages only +3.2 over
sixteen seasons. **Yuan Shao is the only seat that has low pressure *and* bulk *and* a
way out at the same time** — and all three come from how the scenario is laid out.

## The cliff at the end is not conquest

The season-by-season traces show the hegemon swallowing 20–36 commanderies in its last
two seasons (seed 2: 54 → 82 → 92). This was checked: **they were not taken by
fighting.** Across six games the totals are 1207 cities stormed (90%), 113 opened when
provisions ran out (8%), and 27 isolated commanderies submitting (2%) — while the
events in those spike seasons read `submitted｜liuzhang 举 19 郡来降` (Liu Zhang
submits with 19 commanderies). That is the content pack's established "hegemonic
submission" (霸业归降) endgame mechanism, designed for the long tail of one-commandery
factions (the full argument is in the pack under `constants.归降.为何需要`), and it
takes control of seven provinces to reach it.

So the cliff is the **wrap-up**, not the disease. The watershed is seasons 2–16: Yuan
Shao goes 13 → 27 commanderies, while the runner-up Liu Zhang goes 11 → 15. By the
time hegemonic submission triggers, the outcome was long since decided.

## What was not done, and why

- **No numeric penalty was added to the hegemon (reduced output, reduced orders,
  combat-strength discount).** The content pack's
  `constants.联军.为何是绝盟而不是加惩罚` ("why it is breaking alliances rather than
  adding a penalty") records this: experiments plumbed through the combat-strength pipe
  have already come out flat four times in this repo. This round's order-budget sweep
  was the fifth.
- **No diminishing returns were added to garrison output.** The diminishing-returns
  curve on gold has a full empirical argument behind it; garrison does not, and the
  shapes really are asymmetric. But this round has already shown that the bots do not
  spend and that gold is not a constraint, so existing self-play cannot separate "put a
  brake on troops" from "make large factions harder" — it would hold down every seat at
  once, while the monopoly comes from a ratio between seats. A measurement that can tell
  those two apart has to come first.
- **The scenario was not touched.** The 194 layout has a source citation for every
  commandery (`tools/build-scenario-194.mjs`, 482 lines, with control/certainty/basis
  all present). Changing it for balance means trading historical fact for feel, and
  this project's map and household figures are both built on cited sources.

## Two roads available (neither taken yet)

1. **A second scenario.** On the eve of Guandu (官渡) in the fifth year of Jian'an
   (建安五年, 200 CE) only eight or nine factions remain, and it is a two-power
   standoff — for evaluation purposes, a two-power configuration tests judgment better
   than a thirty-one-way free-for-all. The pipeline is already data-driven (the engine
   eats a bundle and does not care about the year), but the scenario itself needs the
   per-commandery source work redone, plus an officer-in-post table and household decay
   coefficients, plus roughly 35 code paths and one menu.
2. **Accept it and document it accurately.** The monopoly is a property of this
   scenario, not a rules bug; state it plainly in the README and stop turning knobs
   over and over.

This round takes the documentation half of option 2; option 1 is left pending a
decision.
